The problem

Agents act. The systems they act on were built for people.

An AI agent differs from a chatbot in one way that matters: it does things. When the only way in is the customer's own login, every organisation it touches inherits four problems at once.

All or nothing

No limits

With a password the agent can do anything the customer can: pay anyone, change details, cancel cover.

Invisible

No way to tell who acted

Your systems see the customer, not the agent. Fraud models and audit trails cannot separate the two.

Unstoppable

No off switch

The only way to stop an agent is a password change, which breaks every other channel the customer uses.

Unaccountable

No evidence

After a loss nobody can prove what the customer actually allowed, so the dispute lands on whoever is easiest to blame.

Nearly every AI identity product solves one organisation's problem: listing and permissioning the agents it builds itself. The agents that matter most to a bank arrive from outside: a customer's assistant, a broker's quoting bot, a partner insurer's claims agent. Your own registry has never heard of them.

Veridact · how it works

Six steps from verified organisation to trusted agent.

The model that made every website verifiable, applied to AI agents. People make the decisions that carry accountability; everything else is automatic.

1 · System

Verify the organisation

The operator proves it controls its domain, the way a website earns its padlock, and for higher assurance that it is a registered company.

2 · Person

Delegate to the agent

An appointed approver sets what the agent may do and spend, and signs it with a passkey. Two people above R1,000.

3 · System

Issue a short-lived credential

The agent receives a verifiable credential that lasts hours, bound to its own key. Every issuance is written to a public log.

4 · Agent

Present it at any member's door

Any bank or insurer challenges the agent and verifies the answer inside its own systems. No prior deal with the operator, and no call to Veridact.

5 · System

Check it may act

The credential shows what the agent was authorised to do. Anything over its limits goes to a person, and Valet takes over for what the customer allowed.

6 · System

Renew or withdraw

Credentials renew automatically. Your own systems can block an agent at once; a withdrawal reaches every member within minutes through a published status list.

No call home

If Veridact goes down, your agents keep working.

Verification runs inside each bank on signed, cached files. Members can mirror every published file, and no single operator, including us, can switch the network off.

Build or share

Keep the decisions in-house. Share the plumbing.

Banks already share clearing, ATM networks and crime intelligence, and compete on products. Agent identity belongs in the same category. Your customers, risk decisions and fraud models never leave you.

Assurance tiers

Free where it can be automated.

Domain-level credentials are free and automatic, so a startup's agent is recognised from day one. Organisation checks, where someone has to verify a company and its approvers, are paid.

Why not just "implement open standards"?

The standards are the envelope. Valet is the form inside it.

OAuth, FAPI 2.0, RAR and DPoP say how to carry a permission. They leave each organisation to define what "pay my bills" means. Ten organisations that each define it differently mean ten integrations for every agent, and most agents won't bother.

Why not build your own?

You'd be building the easy half.

One organisation can build a consent screen. What it cannot build alone is the half that makes it worth having: agents integrating once for everyone, verified agent identity, and decisions no standard makes for you, such as instant pause, rand limits and single-use approvals.

Why trust it?

You run it. You can read every line.

Valet is an open-source project, not a hosted service. You deploy it next to the identity platform you already have, keep your data and keys, and can fork it if you ever need to.

Valet · how it works

Connect once. Act inside the limits. Approve the exceptions. Stop at any time. Prove what happened.

The example below is banking. The same five moments apply to an insurer, a medical scheme, a telecoms provider or a municipality.

1 · Connect

On your screen, never the agent's

The agent asks for access. The customer lands on your own app or website, sees which verified organisation runs the agent, chooses what it may do, and confirms the way they already sign in.

Thandi chooses "Bill payer: existing beneficiaries, up to R2,000 a month, for 90 days".

2 · Act

Checked on every request

The customer's permission is tied to the agent's own cryptographic key at the moment they agree, so a copied token or code is useless to anyone else. Your systems check the mandate, the limits and whether access is still on, every single time.

Kaya pays the water bill. Within the mandate, so it goes through.

3 · Approve

The customer decides the exceptions

Anything outside the mandate goes to the customer in your app, showing exactly what will happen. One approval covers one action, once.

City Power is a new recipient. Thandi approves R1,250 in her bank app.

4 · Stop

One tap, instantly

Pause or disconnect any agent from your app. The very next request is refused, while every other channel keeps working.

Thandi taps "Pause Kaya". Kaya's keys stop working at once.

5 · Prove

The agent is never its own witness.

Each request, approval and refusal becomes a signed record, chained so that changing any one of them shows. In your app, one screen per payment answers the four questions a dispute turns on: which agent asked, what the customer allowed, whose decision it was, and what you did, with your own payment reference.

A receipt is evidence, not a verdict. Who carries a loss stays with your terms and the law; what changes is that nobody is arguing from the agent's version of events.

Start small

Three settings. You decide how far each agent may go.

Saying yes to agents is not one decision. It is a ladder, and your organisation chooses which customers and which agents may reach each rung.

1 · View only

Information first

The agent can read balances and transactions. Nothing can move.

"What did I spend on electricity this year?"

2 · Approve every payment

The agent asks, the customer decides

The agent can ask to pay someone the customer has already saved. Every payment comes back to the customer in your app.

"Pay my water bill." Thandi approves it on her phone.

3 · Standing limit

Routine bills without a tap

For agents you have verified: saved payees only, up to a monthly limit the customer sets. Anything else still comes back for approval.

Kaya pays the water bill inside Thandi's R2,000 a month.

What about the assistants people already use?

They come in through your own front door.

Mainstream assistants connect through a gateway you run, using the Model Context Protocol. They sit on the first two rungs: they can read, and they can ask. In the proof of concept this works with Claude.

Who is really acting?

We only claim what can be verified.

Behind a big assistant platform, your systems can identify the platform, not an individual agent. Valet says so, and holds those assistants to approval for every payment. A standing limit is for agents that prove who they are with their own key.

What if the assistant is tricked?

The rules do not depend on the AI behaving.

An instruction hidden in a payment reference can mislead a model. It cannot make your systems pay someone the customer never saved, raise a limit, or skip an approval.

What Valet holds to

Six rules, each one built into the code.

Never collect passwords

An agent gets its own key, issued by you. It never sees a password, PIN or one-time code.

Write the authority down first

Before an agent acts, the customer sees which payees, how much, how often and for how long.

Keep the rules outside the model

An AI can be misled by a hidden instruction. Your systems can't be talked out of a limit.

The agent is never its own witness

You keep signed, tamper-evident receipts of everything it asked for and everything you decided.

Never touch the money

Valet decides and records. Your own payment system moves the money, and a retry or a timeout can never pay twice.

You stay in charge

Valet is open infrastructure you run yourself, on the identity platform you already have.

Who does what

Valet supplies the rules and the parts. You make every decision.

Valet is a project, not a hosted service. There is no Valet cloud between you, your customers and their agents.

The Valet project provides

  • A shared permission standard: access levels, approval rules, error codes and the receipt format
  • Reference components: grant service, checks on every request, approval flow, payment records that cannot pay twice, receipt chain
  • Screen designs for consent, approvals and the customer's list of connected agents
  • An SDK, so agent builders integrate once for every organisation
  • Adapters for identity platforms, and tests that prove a deployment behaves

Your organisation decides and runs

  • Customer sign-in and step-up, on your existing identity platform
  • Which access levels, limits and durations you offer
  • Which agents you admit, and when you withdraw trust
  • Your brand, your app and your notifications
  • Moving the money, on your own payment system; Valet never executes a payment
  • Keys, data, retention and POPIA obligations; fraud, disputes, support and regulators

Works with the identity platform you already run

Any OpenID Connect provider can sign your customers in. Platforms that support the agent-side standards can also issue the agent's keys themselves. Proven end to end on Keycloak so far; the others are designed for and still to be proven, with PingFederate next.

Questions
What people ask first

    

      
Veridact

      
Why not build agent identity ourselves?
You could, and it would recognise exactly the agents you onboard yourself. Operators will not register separately with every bank and insurer, nobody joins a competitor's scheme, and the moment your agents call a partner you need to federate anyway. Veridact lets you keep every risk decision in-house while sharing only the plumbing that has to be shared.

      
Doesn't a shared network help our competitors?
Only with what is already public. The network shares one fact: which organisation operates an agent, and what it delegated. No customer data, onboarding results, fraud signals or risk decisions ever leave your bank. Think of the padlock on a website: every bank relies on the same certificate system, and none gives anything away by doing so.

      
What happens if Veridact goes down?
Your verification keeps running on cached, signed files, and members can run their own mirrors. Existing credentials stay valid for hours, and agents can renew from another accredited issuer. An outage degrades over hours, not seconds.

      
Does Veridact see our customers or our traffic?
No. Verification happens inside your systems. Credentials and the public log describe organisations, roles and opaque agent identifiers, never customers. Customer consent stays in your own systems.

      
There are many AI identity products. How is Veridact different?
Most work inside one organisation: they register and control the agents a company builds, and their trust ends at that company's boundary. Veridact is built for the space between organisations. It identifies an agent arriving from another company, proves which organisation answers for it and who authorised it, and lets you verify all of that locally.

      
Valet

      
Is Valet a hosted service?
No. Valet is an open-source project (Apache 2.0) that your organisation deploys and runs in its own environment. Your customers' data and your keys never leave you. Commercial support, integration and assurance are available from IAME (IAM Experts) if you want them.

      
Do we have to let outside agents into our systems?
No. Many organisations will start with their own AI assistants, which need exactly the same mandates, limits, approvals and evidence. Opening to verified outside agents becomes a policy decision later, not a rebuild.

      
Is it only for banks?
No. Banking is the first use case because the risk is clearest there. The same model fits insurers, medical schemes, telecoms providers, utilities, municipalities and anyone else whose customers will send agents to act on their accounts.

 
We already run an identity platform. Do we have to replace it?
No. Valet works with what you have. Any OpenID Connect provider can sign customers in, and platforms that support the agent-side standards can issue the agent's keys themselves. Today that is proven end to end on Keycloak; other platforms are designed for and still to be proven.

 
We already have open banking. What does Valet add?
Open banking answers which accredited company may access an account. Valet answers which agent may do what, right now, on whose instruction, and what you can show afterwards. It is an agent profile on the same consent model, not a second consent system: it adds payee, amount and approval rules, enforcement on every request, and a signed record of each action.

 
Our identity vendor has an agent product. Why Valet?
Valet does not compete with your identity platform; it runs on it. The platform signs customers in and issues tokens. Valet adds the rules for acting on an account, enforces them on every request, and keeps the record inside your boundary, in open code you can audit, whichever vendor you use.

 
Does Valet move the money?
No. Valet decides whether a payment may go ahead, holds the amount against the customer's limit and records the outcome. Your own payment system executes it. If that system is slow to answer, the payment is treated as unconfirmed, never as failed, so a retry cannot pay twice.

 
Does a receipt decide who is liable?
No. A receipt shows which agent asked, what the customer allowed, whether the customer approved that exact payment, and what you did. Liability is for your terms, your agreement with the agent's operator and the law. The receipts are the evidence those rely on.

 
Does Valet make an AI agent safe?
Valet doesn't make the AI smarter. It makes its mistakes containable. An agent can still be misled, but it can only act inside the mandate the customer chose, anything else goes back to the customer, and every decision leaves evidence. A payment made inside a standing limit is recorded as exactly that: within what the customer allowed, which is not the same as proof that the customer asked for it.
